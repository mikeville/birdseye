<task>An interactive web prototype where the user looks down at Earth from a viewpoint fixed in inertial space, watching the ground rotate past beneath them, with an altitude slider that changes how fast the motion feels.</task>

<context>
- Product: a tiny single-page thought-experiment toy — make the user viscerally feel the planet rotating beneath them (~1,670 km/h at the equator)
- Audience: curious general users
</context>

<aesthetic>
- Vibe: Chrome dinosaur game — flat, austere, slightly retro. Abstract top-down sketch, NOT a literal map
- Palette: background #f7f7f7, all marks #535353, secondary HUD text #999999
- Typography: monospace only (Courier New / system mono). HUD labels UPPERCASE 10px with 1px letter-spacing; values 14px
- Bans: no Inter, no Roboto, no generic SaaS gradients, no stock blue-to-purple, no real map tiles, no shadows, no gradients on shapes, no rounded decorative elements, no icon libraries
</aesthetic>

<layout>
- Full-viewport canvas; procedural abstract ground scrolls past
- Dead center: small crosshair inside a thin circle outline — the fixed inertial viewpoint, never moves
- Top-left HUD stack: ALTITUDE / FIELD OF VIEW / GROUND SPEED
- Top-right: a 40px thin circle containing "N ↑"
- Bottom-center: altitude slider (1 km → 10,000 km, log scale) with tick labels
- Bottom-left, two lines of small gray text: "Looking down at Earth from a point fixed in space. Earth rotates eastward beneath you — the ground appears to drift right."
</layout>

<interaction>
- Ground drifts LEFT → RIGHT continuously (Earth rotates east beneath the fixed window)
- Altitude slider scales the field of view: low altitude = dizzying speed, high altitude = gentle planetary turn
- Ground is procedurally tiled abstract marks (dots, short rectangles, line segments, dot clusters) — infinite, never obviously repeating, never geographic
</interaction>

<constraints>
- Single HTML file, vanilla canvas + JS, no build step, no dependencies
- No geolocation, no real maps, no time-warp, no latitude control — assume equator throughout
- Default slider position low enough that motion is immediately visible on load
</constraints>

<success_criteria>
On load, the user thinks "wait — that's how fast I'm moving right now?" Zooming out feels calm and planetary; zooming in feels visceral and slightly dizzying.
</success_criteria>
